The Kitchen Doctrine

Ancient Elements.
Fermentative Alchemy.

We reject gas ranges, combi-steamers, and synthetic additives. Our kitchen is an elemental laboratory governed by raw physical thermal physics, microbial biochemistry, and total circularity.

Tripartite Culinary Doctrine

The Three Pillars of Our Hearth

Every single mouthful placed before our guests is informed by this non-negotiable discipline.

01 Thermal Physics

Elemental Fire

We fuel our open hearth solely with Kishu Binchotan—aged Japanese holm oak fired in earthen kilns for three weeks until it achieves near-metallic carbon density.

At temperatures exceeding 900°C, it emits zero acrid soot, delivering pure, radiant far-infrared wavelengths that sear proteins instantaneously while trapping cellular moisture.

02 Subterranean Microbiology

The Fermentation Lab

Two floors below our dining counter lies our hermetically calibrated microbial vaults, where temperature and relative humidity replicate old-growth forest undergrowth.

Here, strains of Aspergillus oryzae propagate on organic Danish pearl barley, creating the enzymatic engine for our 24-month game-meat garums, wild elderflower vinegars, and aged amino pastes.

03 Circular Alchemy

Zero-Waste Gastronomy

Nothing biological leaves our kitchen as waste. Every peel, shell, bone, and spent ferment grain undergoes thermal or microbiological conversion.

Carrot peelings become lacto-fermented glazes; langoustine carapaces yield aromatic cold-pressed finishing oils; spent binchotan ash is purified to season salt crusts.

Service Choreography

Behind the Pass: The Nightly Ballet

From the afternoon ember ignition to the midnight hearth quietude, every movement is choreographed with silent clockwork precision.

17:30
Act I · Ignition

The Kindling of the Coals

The brigade gathers around the hearth. White oak logs are ignited with cedar shavings. The embers are built into three discrete thermal zones: searing heat (900°C), roasting zone (450°C), and the slow-hanging smoking tier (75°C).

18:15
Act II · The Vaults

Decanting the Garum Archives

Subterranean clay jars are uncorked. The Head Fermenter draws drops of aged wild venison garum through glass pipettes, verifying titratable acidity and glutamate concentration before service.

19:00
Act III · The Pass

Synchronized Tweezer Plating

The first guest is seated. Words disappear, replaced by eye contact and quiet hand signals. Six chefs plate twelve courses in exact unison, each placement guided by balance, texture, and thermal velocity.

20:30
Act IV · The Climax

Tableside Embers & Dashi Ritual

A glowing binchotan briquette is immersed directly into an infusion of 10-year kombu broth at the counter, releasing aromatic birch vapor before the final savory movement.
